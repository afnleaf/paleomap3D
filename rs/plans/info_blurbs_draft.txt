info tab blurbs (rough draft v2)
================================
one blurb per map index (109 total). short, event-dense, no taxa.
each entry: index, era + stage + age (verbatim from MAP_NAMES in hud.js),
1-2 sentences of geological/oceanographic/tectonic events, wikipedia url.
URLs are best-guess; a few may need verification (especially Silurian epochs).


0  Present-day  |  Holocene, 0 Ma
   Modern continental configuration; Greenland and Antarctic ice sheets are the only major remnants of Pleistocene glaciation, the Mediterranean is fully open, and the Isthmus of Panama is closed.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Holocene

1  Early Pliocene  |  Zanclean, 4.47 Ma
   The Zanclean megaflood refills the Mediterranean after ~700 ka of evaporite deposition (Messinian salinity crisis); the Isthmus of Panama is shoaling, beginning to redirect global ocean circulation.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Zanclean

2  Middle/Late Miocene  |  Serravallian & Tortonian, 10.5 Ma
   The Mediterranean is being progressively cut off as Africa and Iberia close the Betic and Rifian gateways. C4 grasslands expand worldwide and the East Antarctic Ice Sheet is now permanent.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tortonian

3  Middle Miocene  |  Langhian, 14.9 Ma
   The Miocene Climatic Optimum peaks then collapses; the East Antarctic Ice Sheet expands sharply. The Paratethys begins fragmenting into the Black, Caspian, and Aral basins.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Langhian

4  Early Miocene  |  Aquitanian & Burdigalian, 19.5 Ma
   Africa-Eurasia contact closes the Tethys Seaway, ending circumglobal equatorial circulation. The Red Sea begins opening as Arabia rifts away; the Alps and Himalayas rise rapidly.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Burdigalian

5  Late Oligocene  |  Chattian, 25.6 Ma
   Drake Passage is fully open; the Antarctic Circumpolar Current isolates Antarctica thermally. Antarctic ice sheets oscillate through obliquity-driven cycles.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chattian

6  Early Oligocene  |  Rupelian, 31 Ma
   The Eocene-Oligocene transition (Oi-1) crashes global temperatures; permanent Antarctic ice forms for the first time since the Mesozoic. Sea level drops ~70 m.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rupelian

7  Late Eocene  |  Priabonian, 35.9 Ma
   The Popigai (Siberia) and Chesapeake Bay impact craters form within a few hundred ka of each other. Tropical Tethyan circulation is breaking down as the Indian-Asian collision steepens.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Priabonian

8  Late Middle Eocene  |  Bartonian, 39.5 Ma
   The Azolla event of the Arctic, where dense freshwater fern blooms drew down massive CO2, has finished its ~800 ka pulse. India-Asia collision intensifies, building the proto-Himalayas.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bartonian

9  Early Middle Eocene  |  Lutetian, 44.5 Ma
   Tropical-style climates reach Canada and Antarctica; the planet remains effectively ice-free. The Australian Plate accelerates northward as it separates from Antarctica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lutetian

10 Early Eocene  |  Ypresian, 51.9 Ma
   The Early Eocene Climatic Optimum, the warmest sustained interval of the Cenozoic. Multiple smaller hyperthermals (ETM-2, H-events) punctuate an otherwise hot, ice-free world.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ypresian

11 Paleocene/Eocene Boundary  |  PETM, 56 Ma
   The Paleocene-Eocene Thermal Maximum: ~5 to 8 °C global warming over a few thousand years from a massive carbon release; deep-sea benthic foraminifera suffer the largest extinction of the Cenozoic; Arctic land bridges open as warming peaks.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paleocene%E2%80%93Eocene_Thermal_Maximum

12 Paleocene  |  Danian & Thanetian, 61 Ma
   Recovery interval after the K-Pg extinction. The Dan-C2 hyperthermal (~65.2 Ma) and the Latest Danian Event (~62.2 Ma) are smaller carbon-cycle pulses preceding the PETM.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Danian

13 KT Boundary  |  Latest Maastrichtian, 66 Ma
   The Chicxulub asteroid impact in Yucatán, combined with the climax of Deccan Traps volcanism, drives the Cretaceous-Paleogene mass extinction; ~75% of species vanish geologically instantly.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cretaceous%E2%80%93Paleogene_extinction_event

14 Late Cretaceous  |  Maastrichtian, 69 Ma
   The Western Interior Seaway is draining as the Laramide orogeny lifts the Rocky Mountains; Deccan Traps volcanism in India ramps up toward the K-Pg climax.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Maastrichtian

15 Late Cretaceous  |  Late Campanian, 75 Ma
   The Western Interior Seaway approaches maximum extent, splitting North America into Laramidia and Appalachia. The Pacific Farallon Plate subducts rapidly under western North America, building the Sevier fold-and-thrust belt.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanian

16 Late Cretaceous  |  Early Campanian, 80.8 Ma
   The Laramide orogeny begins flat-slab subduction along western North America. India is still south of the equator, racing northward at unusually fast plate-tectonic speeds (~15 cm/yr).
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Campanian

17 Late Cretaceous  |  Santonian & Coniacian, 86.7 Ma
   Sea level approaches its Phanerozoic peak (~250 m above today). Madagascar separates from India; the Caribbean Plate begins forming over the Galápagos hotspot.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Santonian

18 Mid-Cretaceous  |  Turonian, 91.9 Ma
   The hottest sustained interval of the Phanerozoic; tropical sea-surface temperatures reach 35 to 37 °C. OAE 2 (Bonarelli event) buries vast organic carbon, sourcing many modern oil reservoirs.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Turonian

19 Mid-Cretaceous  |  Cenomanian, 97.2 Ma
   The Western Interior Seaway connects the Arctic and Gulf of Mexico across North America. The Caribbean Large Igneous Province begins erupting on the Pacific seafloor.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cenomanian

20 Early Cretaceous  |  Late Albian, 102.6 Ma
   The South and North Atlantic finally link as Africa and South America fully separate; OAE 1d marks another carbon burial event. Madagascar-India is still attached to Antarctica-Australia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albian

21 Early Cretaceous  |  Middle Albian, 107 Ma
   The Kerguelen Plateau LIP erupts beneath the southern Indian Ocean. The Tethys is closing in the east as India accelerates north; carbonate platforms ring its margins.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albian

22 Early Cretaceous  |  Early Albian, 111 Ma
   Australia-Antarctica is rifting; the South Atlantic remains a young narrow seaway. OAE 1b records oxygen-poor conditions in the Tethys and proto-Atlantic.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Albian

23 Early Cretaceous  |  Late Aptian, 115.8 Ma
   The Nigeria-Brazil rift breaches its restriction; thick salt deposits accumulate in the proto-South Atlantic, forming today's pre-salt oil reservoirs off Brazil and Angola.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptian

24 Early Cretaceous  |  Early Aptian, 121.8 Ma
   OAE 1a (Selli event), one of the largest Mesozoic carbon perturbations, driven by Ontong Java Plateau volcanism in the Pacific (the largest LIP in Earth history by volume).
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aptian

25 Early Cretaceous  |  Barremian, 127.2 Ma
   The South Atlantic begins opening at its southern tip as Africa and South America rift apart. The Faraoni event records short-lived oceanic anoxia in the Tethys.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Barremian

26 Early Cretaceous  |  Hauterivian, 131.2 Ma
   The Weissert event's cooling tail continues; the central Atlantic widens steadily. The Sundance Sea has fully retreated from western North America.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hauterivian

27 Early Cretaceous  |  Valanginian, 136.4 Ma
   The Weissert event: a sharp negative carbon isotope excursion driven by Paraná-Etendeka LIP volcanism, which is also opening the southern South Atlantic.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Valanginian

28 Early Cretaceous  |  Berriasian, 142.4 Ma
   Pangaea has clearly split into Laurasia and Gondwana; the central Atlantic continues to widen. Iberia is rotating, opening the Bay of Biscay.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Berriasian

29 Jurassic/Cretaceous Boundary  |  145 Ma
   A poorly defined boundary with regional turnover but no clear global mass extinction. The Tithonian-Berriasian transition remains the last unresolved system boundary in the Phanerozoic.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tithonian

30 Late Jurassic  |  Tithonian, 148.6 Ma
   The Solnhofen lagoonal limestones (Bavaria) and Morrison floodplains (western US) accumulate. The Hispanic Corridor links the Tethys and Pacific across what is now the Caribbean.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tithonian

31 Late Jurassic  |  Kimmeridgian, 154.7 Ma
   The Kimmeridge Clay (today's North Sea oil source rock) accumulates in restricted, anoxic epicontinental seas; sea level is at a Late Jurassic peak.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kimmeridgian

32 Late Jurassic  |  Oxfordian, 160.4 Ma
   Pangaea has clearly split into Laurasia and Gondwana; the Hispanic Corridor opens between them. Madagascar separates from the African mainland.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Oxfordian_(stage)

33 Middle Jurassic  |  Callovian, 164.8 Ma
   Atlantic spreading propagates north toward Iberia. The Sundance Sea floods western North America from the Arctic; carbonate platforms ring the western Tethys.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Callovian

34 Middle Jurassic  |  Bajocian & Bathonian, 168.2 Ma
   The Karoo-Ferrar LIP aftermath continues to drive Pangaea apart; Gondwana begins to fragment. The Central Atlantic now hosts a true mid-ocean ridge.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bajocian

35 Middle Jurassic  |  Aalenian, 172.2 Ma
   Spreading begins between East and West Gondwana along the proto-Indian Ocean. Sea level rises through a long Jurassic transgression after the Toarcian crisis.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Aalenian

36 Early Jurassic  |  Toarcian, 178.4 Ma
   The Toarcian Oceanic Anoxic Event (TOAE), driven by Karoo-Ferrar LIP volcanism in southern Gondwana; black shales accumulate widely with ~3 °C ocean warming.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Toarcian

37 Early Jurassic  |  Pliensbachian, 186.8 Ma
   Cooler interval following the Sinemurian-Pliensbachian boundary event. The Central Atlantic Magmatic Province has finished erupting; Gondwana is just beginning to break.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pliensbachian

38 Early Jurassic  |  Sinemurian/Pliensbachian, 190.8 Ma
   The Sinemurian-Pliensbachian boundary event records a brief carbon-cycle perturbation. Tethys Ocean shelves deposit thick carbonate platforms.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sinemurian

39 Early Jurassic  |  Hettangian & Sinemurian, 196 Ma
   Sea levels rise sharply after the end-Triassic regression. Reefs, which had collapsed at the boundary, slowly rebuild on Tethyan margins.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hettangian

40 Late Triassic  |  Rhaetian/Hettangian, 201.3 Ma
   The end-Triassic mass extinction. The Central Atlantic Magmatic Province (CAMP) is the largest known LIP by area, ripping Pangaea open and devastating reefs and conodont faunas.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Triassic%E2%80%93Jurassic_extinction_event

41 Late Triassic  |  Rhaetian, 204.9 Ma
   Pangaea is at maximum assembly and just starting to crack along the future Atlantic; CAMP eruptions are imminent. Sea level rises after the prolonged Triassic regression.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Rhaetian

42 Late Triassic  |  Late Norian, 213.2 Ma
   The Manicouagan impact (Quebec) leaves a 100 km crater. Pangaea's interior is hyperarid; widespread evaporites accumulate in restricted basins.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norian

43 Late Triassic  |  Mid Norian, 217.8 Ma
   The Wrangellia LIP erupts on what is now the Pacific margin of North America. Tethyan reef ecosystems are still recovering from the Carnian Pluvial collapse.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norian

44 Late Triassic  |  Early Norian, 222.4 Ma
   Aftermath of the Carnian Pluvial Event; arid conditions return to Pangaea's interior. The Newark Supergroup rift basins of eastern North America accumulate thick lacustrine sequences.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Norian

45 Late Triassic  |  Carnian/Norian, 227 Ma
   Aftermath of the Carnian Pluvial Event drives global ecosystem reorganization. Calcareous nannoplankton (coccoliths) appear in the rock record, beginning a transformation of ocean chemistry.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnian

46 Late Triassic  |  Carnian, 232 Ma
   The Carnian Pluvial Event: a ~1 Myr humid interval likely tied to Wrangellia LIP volcanism; reef ecosystems collapse and arid red beds give way to coal-forming wetlands.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnian

47 Late Triassic  |  Early Carnian, 233.6 Ma
   Onset of the Carnian Pluvial Event. Pangaea remains assembled but with monsoonal climates intensifying; the carbonate reefs of the Tethys (Dolomites) thrive just before collapse.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Carnian

48 Middle Triassic  |  Ladinian, 239.5 Ma
   Conifers replace lycopsid relics as the dominant flora in many regions. Carbonate platforms continue building along the western Tethys.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ladinian

49 Middle Triassic  |  Anisian, 244.6 Ma
   Recovery from the Permian-Triassic extinction is well underway; reefs return after a ~10 Myr "reef gap." Pangaea remains fully assembled with the Tethys wedged into its eastern flank.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Anisian

50 Permo-Triassic Boundary  |  252 Ma
   The Great Dying. Siberian Traps volcanism erupts millions of cubic kilometers of basalt, acidifies the oceans, and strips ozone; ~96% of marine species and ~70% of terrestrial vertebrate genera disappear.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Permian%E2%80%93Triassic_extinction_event

51 Late Permian  |  Lopingian, 256 Ma
   The world just before the Great Dying. Pangaea is fully assembled, the interior is hyperarid, and the Siberian Traps are about to erupt at the easternmost margin of the supercontinent.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lopingian

52 Late Middle Permian  |  Capitanian, 262.5 Ma
   The Capitanian (Olson's) extinction, driven by Emeishan Traps volcanism in what is now South China; reefs of the Permian Basin (West Texas) thrive on the opposite side of Pangaea.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Capitanian

53 Middle Permian  |  Wordian/Capitanian Boundary, 265.1 Ma
   The Permian Basin reefs of West Texas (Capitan Reef) accumulate huge carbonate platforms. Glaciation has fully retreated from Gondwana.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wordian

54 Middle Permian  |  Roadian & Wordian, 268.7 Ma
   Pangaea is fully assembled with the Tethys wedged into its eastern flank. The Late Paleozoic Ice Age has ended; Earth enters a new greenhouse state.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Roadian

55 Early Permian  |  Late Kungurian, 275 Ma
   The Late Paleozoic Ice Age is in retreat; glaciers vanishing from Gondwana. Hyperarid evaporite basins (Zechstein, Russian Platform) accumulate vast salt and gypsum deposits.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kungurian

56 Early Permian  |  Early Kungurian, 280 Ma
   Walchian conifer forests of the seasonally dry Pangaean interior signal the end of the great Carboniferous coal swamps. Glaciation is in retreat over Gondwana.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kungurian

57 Early Permian  |  Artinskian, 286.8 Ma
   The Cisuralian icehouse loosens; ice sheets retreat from Australia and South America. Permian red beds (Texas, Russia) record drying continental interiors.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Artinskian

58 Early Permian  |  Sakmarian, 292.6 Ma
   Pangaea fully assembled; the Late Paleozoic Ice Age is still at high intensity over Gondwana. Glossopteris flora dominates southern continents.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sakmarian

59 Early Permian  |  Asselian, 297 Ma
   The Carboniferous-Permian boundary. The Late Paleozoic Ice Age is near its peak; cyclothems record glacioeustatic sea-level swings across Euramerica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Asselian

60 Late Pennsylvanian  |  Gzhelian, 301.3 Ma
   Pangaea is essentially assembled; the Variscan, Alleghenian, and Hercynian orogenies have welded its central spine. Gondwanan ice sheets are at maximum.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Gzhelian

61 Late Pennsylvanian  |  Kasimovian, 305.4 Ma
   The Carboniferous Rainforest Collapse: equatorial coal swamps fragment as climates dry. Gondwanan glaciation pulses to peak intensity.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kasimovian

62 Middle Pennsylvanian  |  Moscovian, 311.1 Ma
   Equatorial coal swamps blanket Euramerica at maximum extent. Glacioeustatic cyclothems leave the alternating coal-shale-limestone sequences of the American Midwest.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Moscovian_(Carboniferous)

63 Early/Middle Carboniferous  |  Bashkirian/Moscovian boundary, 314.6 Ma
   The Mississippian-Pennsylvanian transition. Gondwana ice sheets pulse strongly; sea-level cyclothems record the glacial-interglacial swings.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirian

64 Early Pennsylvanian  |  Bashkirian, 319.2 Ma
   The Variscan-Alleghenian orogeny is actively suturing Gondwana to Euramerica, building Pangaea. Tropical coal swamps continue to expand across the equator.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bashkirian

65 Late Mississippian  |  Serpukhovian, 327 Ma
   Glaciation intensifies in southern Gondwana; the Late Paleozoic Ice Age is now in full force. Sea-level cyclothems begin to dominate carbonate platforms.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhovian

66 Late Mississippian  |  Visean/Serpukhovian boundary, 330.9 Ma
   The Mid-Carboniferous boundary. The Variscan orogeny is welding southern Europe and northern Africa together as Gondwana approaches Euramerica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Serpukhovian

67 Middle Mississippian  |  Late Visean, 333 Ma
   Mississippian limestones of Britain, Belgium, and the American Midwest accumulate on broad shallow shelves. Glaciation begins building over high-latitude Gondwana.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visean

68 Middle Mississippian  |  Middle Visean, 338.8 Ma
   The first widespread coal-forming environments appear in Donbass and the British Midlands. Cratonic seas blanket much of Euramerica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visean

69 Middle Mississippian  |  Early Visean, 344 Ma
   The Old Red Sandstone continent (Euramerica) is being eroded into the Carboniferous limestone shelves. Reef-building has shifted from stromatoporoid-coral systems to microbial mounds.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Visean

70 Early Mississippian  |  Late Tournaisian, 349 Ma
   Middle of "Romer's Gap," a fossil-poor interval after the Hangenberg event. Sea level rebounds from the Devonian-Carboniferous lowstand.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournaisian

71 Early Mississippian  |  Early Tournaisian, 354 Ma
   Recovery from the Hangenberg event; ocean anoxia eases. Atmospheric oxygen begins its long Carboniferous climb toward ~30%.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tournaisian

72 Devono-Carboniferous Boundary  |  358.9 Ma
   The Hangenberg event: anoxia, cooling, and a brief Gondwanan glaciation finish the Late Devonian extinctions. Sea levels crash and recover.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hangenberg_event

73 Late Devonian  |  Middle Famennian, 365.6 Ma
   Recovery interval between the Kellwasser and Hangenberg events. Greening of the continents drives soil formation and atmospheric CO2 drawdown.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famennian

74 Late Devonian  |  Early Famennian, 370 Ma
   Aftermath of the Kellwasser event; stromatoporoid-coral reefs are largely gone. The Antler orogeny begins along western Laurentia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Famennian

75 Late Devonian  |  Late Frasnian, 375 Ma
   The Kellwasser event, a two-pulse mass extinction tied to oceanic anoxia and cooling; Devonian reef ecosystems collapse globally.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frasnian

76 Late Devonian  |  Early Frasnian, 380 Ma
   Devonian reefs reach their Phanerozoic peak: the Canning Basin (Australia) and Alberta preserve massive stromatoporoid-coral structures just before collapse.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Frasnian

77 Middle Devonian  |  Givetian, 385.2 Ma
   The Acadian orogeny continues to build the Appalachians as Avalonia collides with Laurussia. The first widespread forests sharply increase continental weathering.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Givetian

78 Middle Devonian  |  Eifelian, 390.5 Ma
   The Choteč and Kačák events record short carbon-cycle perturbations and reef stress. Reef systems and forests both expand toward their late-Devonian peaks.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Eifelian

79 Early Devonian  |  Late Emsian, 395 Ma
   The Daleje event records oceanic anoxia in the Tethys. Continental weathering accelerates as land plants spread inland from coastal margins.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emsian

80 Early Devonian  |  Middle Emsian, 400 Ma
   The Acadian orogeny is welding Avalonia to Laurussia, building eastern North America's Appalachian system. Old Red Sandstone deposition dominates Euramerican lowlands.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emsian

81 Early Devonian  |  Early Emsian, 405 Ma
   The proto-Atlantic Iapetus Ocean has closed; the Caledonian and Acadian orogenies are still actively building mountains. Vascular land plants develop secondary growth and lignin.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Emsian

82 Early Devonian  |  Pragian, 409.2 Ma
   The Rhynie Chert (Scotland), dated to ~407 Ma, preserves an exceptional terrestrial ecosystem in volcanic hot-spring sinters. Land plants are still mostly small (<50 cm).
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pragian

83 Early Devonian  |  Lochkovian, 415 Ma
   The opening of the Devonian, just after the Klonk event near the Silurian-Devonian boundary. The Caledonian orogeny continues to weld Laurentia, Baltica, and Avalonia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lochkovian

84 Late Silurian  |  Pridoli, 421.1 Ma
   The Caledonian orogeny is at its peak as Avalonia fully docks with Laurussia. The Iapetus Ocean closes; the proto-Atlantic disappears.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Pridoli_epoch

85 Late Silurian  |  Ludlow, 425.2 Ma
   The Lau event records oceanic anoxia and a positive carbon isotope excursion. Tethyan and Laurussian shelves continue to host coral-stromatoporoid reefs.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ludlow_(epoch)

86 Middle Silurian  |  Wenlock, 430.4 Ma
   The Mulde event, a positive carbon isotope excursion paired with graptolite extinctions. Coral-stromatoporoid reefs flourish in the warm Wenlock seas.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wenlock_epoch

87 Early Silurian  |  Late Llandovery, 436 Ma
   The Ireviken event, a positive carbon excursion and conodont-graptolite turnover, closes the early Silurian recovery. Reefs continue to rebuild.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandovery_epoch

88 Early Silurian  |  Early Llandovery, 441.2 Ma
   Aftermath of the end-Ordovician glaciation. Sea levels rise rapidly, drowning continental margins; the first vascular land plants appear in the rock record.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Llandovery_epoch

89 Late Ordovician  |  Hirnantian, 444.5 Ma
   The Hirnantian glaciation, the most intense glaciation of the early Phanerozoic, drives the second-largest mass extinction in Earth's history; ~85% of marine species perish.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Hirnantian

90 Late Ordovician  |  Katian, 449.1 Ma
   Sea levels approach Phanerozoic peaks; cratonic seas blanket Laurentia, Baltica, and northern Gondwana. The Taconic orogeny continues building eastern Laurentia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Katian

91 Late Ordovician  |  Sandbian, 455.7 Ma
   The Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event (GOBE) reaches its zenith. The Taconic orogeny begins as an island arc collides with eastern Laurentia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sandbian

92 Middle Ordovician  |  Late Darriwilian, 460 Ma
   GOBE in full swing. The Iapetus Ocean reaches maximum width as Avalonia drifts north of Gondwana toward Baltica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darriwilian

93 Middle Ordovician  |  Early Darriwilian, 465 Ma
   Reef-building shifts from microbial to skeletal as the first stromatoporoid- and coral-built reefs appear. Cratonic seas blanket much of the continents.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Darriwilian

94 Early Ordovician  |  Floian/Dapingian boundary, 470 Ma
   The Lower-Middle Ordovician transition. Sea levels rise broadly, flooding cratonic interiors; the GOBE accelerates.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floian

95 Early Ordovician  |  Late Early Floian, 475 Ma
   Continental positions are dominated by Gondwana over the south pole. Cratonic shallow seas continue to expand across Laurentia, Baltica, and Siberia.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Floian

96 Early Ordovician  |  Tremadoc, 481.6 Ma
   The opening of the Ordovician. Sea level continues to rise after the Cambrian extinction pulses; the Iapetus and Rheic oceans are widening.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tremadocian

97 Cambro-Ordovician Boundary  |  485.4 Ma
   A series of late Cambrian extinction pulses end the period. Conodont and graptolite faunas reset; the stage is set for the Great Ordovician Biodiversification Event.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian%E2%80%93Ordovician_extinction_event

98 Late Cambrian  |  Jiangshanian, 491.8 Ma
   The Furongian epoch. The SPICE event records a global positive carbon isotope excursion tied to widespread organic carbon burial.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jiangshanian

99 Late Cambrian  |  Paibian, 495.5 Ma
   The Paibian opens with another extinction pulse. Cratonic shelves are flooded by warm shallow seas across most of Gondwana, Laurentia, and Baltica.
   https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Paibian

100 Late Middle Cambrian  |  Guzhangian, 498.8 Ma
    Time of the Burgess Shale (British Columbia) and similar lagerstätten. Cratonic seas remain widespread; reefs are dominated by microbialites.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Guzhangian

101 Late Middle Cambrian  |  Early Epoch 3, 505 Ma
    Archaeocyathid reefs are gone; microbialites and rare metazoan structures dominate reef-building. Sea levels remain high across most cratons.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wuliuan

102 Early Middle Cambrian  |  Late Epoch 2, 510 Ma
    The Sinsk event, an oceanic anoxic event in the Tethyan-Iapetus realm, ends archaeocyathid reefs. Time of the Chengjiang and Sirius Passet biotas.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Series_2

103 Early Middle Cambrian  |  Middle Epoch 2, 515 Ma
    The Cambrian explosion is in full swing. Archaeocyathid reefs ring the warm shallow margins of Gondwana, Laurentia, and Siberia.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Series_2

104 Early/Middle Cambrian boundary  |  520 Ma
    Peak of the Cambrian explosion. Bioturbation transforms seafloor sediments globally, ending the "Ediacaran substrate revolution."
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Cambrian_Series_2

105 Early Cambrian  |  Late Terreneuvian, 525 Ma
    The first archaeocyathid reefs build along Siberian and Gondwanan margins. Small shelly fossils proliferate in the rock record.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terreneuvian

106 Early Cambrian  |  Middle Terreneuvian, 530 Ma
    The "small shelly fossil" radiation. Complex burrows show animals with hydrostatic skeletons; the seafloor "matground" of the Ediacaran is being broken up.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Terreneuvian

107 Early Cambrian  |  Early Terreneuvian, 535 Ma
    The opening pulse of the Cambrian. Trace fossils become deeper and more complex (Treptichnus pedum); biomineralized small shelly fossils first appear.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortunian

108 Cambrian/Precambrian boundary  |  541 Ma
    The base of the Phanerozoic. The soft-bodied Ediacaran biota gives way to a world of skeletons and complex behavior; Treptichnus pedum is the GSSP marker at Fortune Head, Newfoundland.
    https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Fortunian
